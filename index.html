How a review works

Built around the challenge process referees already follow, so a review gets faster without changing who decides.

  1. The referee taps the challenge type

    In or out, a touch at the block, and the other calls coaches can challenge.

  2. Osprey cues the right angle

    The best camera angle opens at the moment that matters, instead of someone scrubbing through footage.

  3. Osprey Vision adds the evidence

    Ball tracking and physics show where the ball landed and the mark it left, or whether its path changed at a block.

Then the referee decides.

Osprey supplies evidence. It never makes or announces a call.

What Osprey Vision shows

Two kinds of challenge decide close sets: where the ball landed, and whether a blocker touched it. Vision adds data points to both, so the referee isn't left judging a freeze-frame.

Where it landed

Osprey Vision tracks the ball to the floor and shows the mark it left against the line, so a line call rests on the contact itself.

Whether the block touched it

Vision compares the ball's path before and after the blocker's hands. A change in direction is evidence of a touch, even when no angle shows the fingertips.
